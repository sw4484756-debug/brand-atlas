# Global competitor discovery

Use this route when competitors are missing or the input is a brand trait, signature product, category or use case.

## Resolve the clue

Identify the entity and category before expanding. A product name can be generic or shared across owners; an unfamiliar brand can have namesakes. Search the exact name and native-language spelling, brand/product owner pages, and relevant market context. State uncertainty or ask a short clarification when it changes the candidate set. Do not present a speculative identity as confirmed.

For traits, translate the clue into a buying need, audience, occasion, channel and rough price tier. For a signature product, find both product-level substitutes and brand-level alternatives; a milk drink may compete with tea or smoothies as well as coffee. Keep these levels explicit.

## Discover across markets

Search multiple useful geographic clusters and their local languages. Prefer the user's target markets; for an unspecified global exploration, choose diverse representative regions appropriate to the category. This is a reasoned sample, not a claim of complete global coverage.

Consider three candidate roles:

- **Direct/local:** same audience, purchase occasion and accessible geography.
- **Substitute:** solves the same job through a different category or format.
- **Strategic reference:** offers a useful positioning contrast but may not sell in the same market.

Check that candidates are real and relevant, and distinguish brand origin from the market actually researched. Record official websites and evidence for products/traits. Briefly justify the shortlist before scoring. Preserve any named competitors from the user.

## Compare with context

Use common, observable dimensions. A global concept map can compare format emphasis, product breadth and documented digital capabilities; absolute price/value, taste, service speed or consumer trust need comparable local measurements. A capability being advertised does not establish adoption or quality. Absence from a search result does not establish absence from the brand.

For each brand include its proposition, audience inference, strengths, specific vulnerabilities or improvement hypotheses, and evidence. Do not add the axis scores into a universal competitiveness number. If the user requests one, agree a purpose and transparent weights, handle missingness and show sensitivity to weights.

## Handoff and updates

The HTML discovery panel filters brands and metadata already in the report. With the optional local workspace it can also queue a research request for the host assistant; see [live-research.md](live-research.md). On “执行研究”, read that queue and the existing brief instead of showing a new intake table. Research the requested additions or changed dimensions, preserve unrelated evidence, and commit the update. Never label cached candidate filtering as live global discovery.

For a large search, keep a candidate ledger (brand, market, role, reason, source) beside the report and render regional subsets of at most eight brands. The final user-facing report should explain what was and was not covered.
